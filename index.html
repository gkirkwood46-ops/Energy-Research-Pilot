Our analysis
We reviewed company-reported financial information and disclosures to identify unusual items, important changes and context that may help explain the numbers. This is our interpretation of the available evidence, not a recommendation.
CNQ — headline earnings need context ⚑Reported 2025 net earnings were $10.82B, but adjusted net earnings from operations were $7.44B. Company reporting identifies $3.376B of net non-operating income in 2025. The headline increase therefore overstates the change in underlying operating earnings.
Source: CNQ 2025 MD&A ↗
Suncor — lower cash generation, lower debtAdjusted funds from operations declined from $13.85B in 2024 to $12.78B in 2025, while net debt declined from $6.86B to $6.34B. Lower cash generation and debt reduction can coexist; neither number should be interpreted alone.
Source: Suncor financial reports ↗
Cenovus — debt jumped, but there is a reasonNet debt increased from $4.61B at the end of 2024 to $8.29B at the end of 2025. Cenovus explicitly says the increase followed closing of the MEG acquisition, partially offset by WRB divestiture proceeds. A red “debt up = bad” signal would miss the event behind the number.
Source: Cenovus FY2025 results ↗
Imperial — unusual items pushed reported earnings down ⚑Reported 2025 net income was $3.27B; net income excluding identified items was $4.30B. Identified items included Norman Wells end-of-life costs, an impairment, restructuring charges and an inventory-related charge. Here the unusual items make reported earnings look worse—the opposite direction from CNQ.
Source: Imperial FY2025 results ↗
